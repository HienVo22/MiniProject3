# Validation Reflection — hvo5

I validated kbissonn's ten MiniProject2 projects: AbstractGPs.jl, ITK-SNAP, SAGE, enterprise, mapxmaps, scikit-fem, GraphScope, Bijectors.jl, pecos, and tuto-fsl. The comparison is 50 rows, five fields on each project. We already agreed on 28 of them. The other 22 are written up as proposals. kbissonn had already turned in a validation log, but it reviews him2, a student from a previous term. He told me he did that because my MiniProject2 repository only had Part 1 when he looked, and he offered to go through this log. Until he confirms the disagreed rows, the post-discussion agreement is the same as the pre-discussion agreement, and NumResolved is 0. Cohen's kappa after discussion is therefore the same number as before.

The long gaps that are real have a recognizable shape. SAGE goes from a handful of commits in 2021 to none in 2022 and 2023, after a stretch of small bug fixes, and pecos thins out to about 11 commits in 2022 before a 15-month hole. What ends those gaps is a specific person coming back to do a chore that could not be put off: Darren Croton ports SAGE from Python 2 to Python 3, and Katherine Klise updates pecos for a new pandas API and replaces nose with pytest. ITK-SNAP's five-month hole in 2010-2011 ends the same way, with pyushkevich returning to burn down bug reports. Recovery here is a maintainer coming back, not a new community appearing.

A gap is not the same thing as the project dying, and a short quiet stretch is not a gap. mapxmaps is the case that looks dead and is dead: 63 months with no commits, then one content edit on 2025-01-22 that cites a NASA direction and an executive order. That commit does not revive the project, which is why I agree with kbissonn that HasRecovered is No and Currentstatus is Inactive. tuto-fsl is the case that looks the same and is not. Its longest hole is four months in 2021, and 68 commits come after it. The repository does stop in June 2022, so it is Inactive now, but it had already recovered from the longest gap. GraphScope, Bijectors.jl, scikit-fem, and AbstractGPs.jl are the other side of the same mistake. Their longest empty stretches are one or two months, GraphScope's is the two months after the repository was opened, and AbstractGPs has no three-month hole in six years. Those were coded as recoveries anyway. AbstractGPs is still a declining project, from 613 commits in 2021 to 37 in 2026. Declining and "never went inactive" both fit.

The coding guide's date rule settled the one Currentstatus disagreement. SAGE's last commit is 2025-11-04. The rule says Inactive only if the last commit is before 2025-04-01, so SAGE is Active even though it sat still for 28 months. pecos lands on 2025-04-03, two days onto the Active side of the same line. I also did not treat Seasonal as an activity pattern, because it is not in the codebook, and I did not treat Cyclical as a fit for enterprise or Bijectors.jl. Their yearly counts bounce, but not on a fixed interval, so both are irregular.

RecentThemes was the weakest field, 3 agreements out of 10. The misses come from reading the theme off the whole history instead of the last 10 commits. pecos's latest commits are release notes and documentation, not bug fixes. GraphScope's latest commits are blog posts and pages rebuilt by github-actions. scikit-fem's latest substantive commits add an example and update docs, and the label "Bug fixing" is not the codebook's "Bug fixes." HasRecovered has a percent agreement of 50 and a kappa of 0. The percent is not a bug in the formula. kbissonn answered Yes on 8 of the 10 projects, so chance agreement is already about 50 percent, and matching on half the rows does not beat chance. The shared mistake underneath it is calling any return to commits a recovery, including returns from a month or two of quiet and including the month a repository was created.

The proposals I would ask him to accept are the ones a rule already decides: no recovery without a gap of at least three months, Currentstatus from the 2025-04-01 cutoff, activity labels restricted to the codebook, and recent themes taken from the last 10 commits. The rows we already match, including every real long-gap recovery except the reason given for pecos, can stay as he wrote them.
